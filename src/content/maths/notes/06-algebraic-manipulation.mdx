# Algebraic manipulation

<Key>
Algebra uses letters for numbers. **Expanding** removes brackets; **factorising** puts them back. They are opposites.
</Key>

## Expanding brackets

Multiply every term inside by the term outside.

$$3(2x - 5) = 6x - 15 \qquad x(x + 4) = x^2 + 4x$$

**Two brackets** (FOIL: first, outer, inner, last):

$$(x + 3)(x - 5) = x^2 - 5x + 3x - 15 = x^2 - 2x - 15$$

Squaring a bracket: $(x + 4)^2 = (x+4)(x+4) = x^2 + 8x + 16$.

<Mistake>
$(x + 4)^2$ is **not** $x^2 + 16$. You must write it out twice and expand.
</Mistake>

## Factorising

1. **Common factor:** $6x^2 + 9x = 3x(2x + 3)$.
2. **Difference of two squares:** $x^2 - 49 = (x - 7)(x + 7)$.
3. **Quadratic** $x^2 + bx + c$: find two numbers that multiply to $c$ and add to $b$. $x^2 + 7x + 12 = (x+3)(x+4)$.
4. **Grouping:** $xy + 3y + 2x + 6 = y(x+3) + 2(x+3) = (x+3)(y+2)$.

<Ext>
Quadratics with a leading coefficient: $2x^2 + 7x + 3 = (2x + 1)(x + 3)$. Find two numbers that multiply to $2 \times 3 = 6$ and add to 7 (they are 1 and 6), split the middle term and factorise by grouping.
</Ext>

## Algebraic fractions

Factorise first, then cancel **factors** (never terms).

$$\frac{x^2 - 9}{x^2 + 3x} = \frac{(x-3)(x+3)}{x(x+3)} = \frac{x - 3}{x}$$

To add, use a common denominator: $\dfrac{2}{x} + \dfrac{3}{x+1} = \dfrac{2(x+1) + 3x}{x(x+1)} = \dfrac{5x + 2}{x(x+1)}$.

## Substitution

Replace letters with numbers and follow BODMAS. If $a = 3$ and $b = -2$: $2a^2 - b = 2(9) - (-2) = 20$.

## Changing the subject

Do the **same operation to both sides**, undoing operations in reverse order.

Make $x$ the subject of $y = 3x - 7$:
$$y + 7 = 3x \quad\Rightarrow\quad x = \frac{y + 7}{3}$$

Make $r$ the subject of $A = \pi r^2$: $r = \sqrt{\dfrac{A}{\pi}}$.

<Tip>
If the new subject appears twice, collect those terms on one side, factorise, then divide.
</Tip>

## Worked example

Factorise fully: $2x^2 - 50$.

$$2x^2 - 50 = 2(x^2 - 25) = 2(x-5)(x+5)$$
