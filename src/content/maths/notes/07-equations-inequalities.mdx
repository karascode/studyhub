# Linear equations and inequalities

<Key>
To solve an equation, keep it **balanced**: whatever you do to one side, do to the other, until the unknown stands alone.
</Key>

## Solving linear equations

$$5x - 7 = 18 \;\Rightarrow\; 5x = 25 \;\Rightarrow\; x = 5$$

**Unknown on both sides:** collect $x$ terms on one side.
$$7x + 2 = 3x + 18 \;\Rightarrow\; 4x = 16 \;\Rightarrow\; x = 4$$

**Brackets:** expand first. $3(x + 2) = 21 \Rightarrow 3x + 6 = 21 \Rightarrow x = 5$.

**Fractions:** multiply every term by the common denominator.
$$\frac{x}{3} + \frac{x}{4} = 7 \;\Rightarrow\; 4x + 3x = 84 \;\Rightarrow\; x = 12$$

## Forming equations

1. Let the unknown be $x$.
2. Write an expression for each quantity.
3. Set them equal and solve.
4. Check your answer in the original wording.

Example: three consecutive numbers add to 78. $x + (x+1) + (x+2) = 78 \Rightarrow 3x + 3 = 78 \Rightarrow x = 25$. The numbers are 25, 26 and 27.

## Inequalities

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $>$ | greater than |
| $\ge$ | greater than or equal to |
| $<$ | less than |
| $\le$ | less than or equal to |

Solve like an equation, with one exception:

<Mistake>
When you **multiply or divide by a negative number**, the inequality sign **reverses**. $-2x < 10$ gives $x > -5$.
</Mistake>

Example: $3x - 4 \le 11 \Rightarrow 3x \le 15 \Rightarrow x \le 5$.

## Number lines

- **Open circle** for $<$ or $>$ (the end value is *not* included).
- **Closed (filled) circle** for $\le$ or $\ge$ (included).

Two inequalities combine: $-2 < x \le 4$ means $x$ is greater than $-2$ and at most 4. Integers satisfying it: $-1, 0, 1, 2, 3, 4$.

## Graphical inequalities

<Ext>
To show $y > 2x + 1$ on a graph, draw the line $y = 2x + 1$ (**dashed** for $>$ or $<$, **solid** for $\ge$ or $\le$), then shade the required side. Test a point such as $(0,0)$ to decide which side.
</Ext>

## Worked example

Solve $\dfrac{2x + 1}{5} = x - 2$.

$$2x + 1 = 5x - 10 \;\Rightarrow\; 11 = 3x \;\Rightarrow\; x = \frac{11}{3}$$
