# Simultaneous equations

<Key>
Two equations with two unknowns are solved **together**. The solution is the pair $(x, y)$ that satisfies both. Graphically it is the **point where the two lines cross**.
</Key>

## Method 1: elimination

1. Make the coefficients of one letter the same (multiply an equation if needed).
2. **Add** the equations if the signs are different; **subtract** if the signs are the same.
3. Solve, then substitute back to find the other letter.

Solve $3x + 2y = 16$ and $x - 2y = 0$.

Add the equations (the $y$ terms cancel): $4x = 16 \Rightarrow x = 4$. Then $4 - 2y = 0 \Rightarrow y = 2$.

Check in equation 1: $3(4) + 2(2) = 16$. ✓

## Method 2: substitution

Useful when one equation already has a letter alone.

$y = 2x - 1$ and $3x + y = 14$.

$$3x + (2x - 1) = 14 \;\Rightarrow\; 5x = 15 \;\Rightarrow\; x = 3,\; y = 5$$

## Method 3: graphic calculator

In 0607 you may use the calculator's **simultaneous equation solver**, or draw both graphs and find the **intersection**. For "show that" or "solve by an algebraic method" questions you must still show working.

<Tip>
Always substitute your answers into the *other* equation to check. It takes ten seconds and catches arithmetic slips.
</Tip>

## Forming simultaneous equations

Two adults and three children pay 31 for tickets. One adult and four children pay 29. Let adult $= a$ and child $= c$:
$$2a + 3c = 31 \qquad a + 4c = 29$$
Multiply the second by 2: $2a + 8c = 58$. Subtract: $5c = 27$, so $c = 5.4$ and $a = 7.4$.

## One linear and one quadratic

<Ext>
Substitute the linear equation into the quadratic to get a quadratic in one letter, then solve.

$y = x + 2$ and $y = x^2$: $x^2 = x + 2 \Rightarrow x^2 - x - 2 = 0 \Rightarrow (x-2)(x+1) = 0$.

So $x = 2, y = 4$ or $x = -1, y = 1$. The line cuts the curve at **two points**.
</Ext>

## Worked example

Solve $2x + y = 11$ and $x - y = 1$.

Add: $3x = 12$, so $x = 4$. Then $y = x - 1 = 3$.
