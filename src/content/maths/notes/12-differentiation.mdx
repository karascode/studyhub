# Differentiation

<Ext>
This topic is **Extended** only.
</Ext>

<Key>
The **derivative** $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ gives the **gradient** of a curve at any point. It also measures the **rate of change** of $y$ with respect to $x$.
</Key>

## Differentiating powers of $x$

$$y = ax^n \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{dy}{dx} = a n x^{n-1}$$

**Multiply by the power, then reduce the power by 1.**

| $y$ | $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ |
|---|---|
| $x^3$ | $3x^2$ |
| $5x^2$ | $10x$ |
| $4x$ | $4$ |
| $7$ (a constant) | $0$ |
| $\dfrac{1}{x} = x^{-1}$ | $-x^{-2} = -\dfrac{1}{x^2}$ |
| $\sqrt{x} = x^{1/2}$ | $\tfrac12 x^{-1/2}$ |

Differentiate **term by term**: $y = 2x^3 - 5x^2 + 3x - 4$ gives $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 6x^2 - 10x + 3$.

<Mistake>
Forgetting to write powers first. Rewrite $\dfrac{3}{x^2}$ as $3x^{-2}$ before differentiating, giving $-6x^{-3}$.
</Mistake>

## Gradient and tangent

1. Differentiate.
2. Substitute the $x$-value to get the gradient $m$ of the **tangent**.
3. Find $y$ at that point and use $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$ for the tangent's equation.

For $y = x^2 + 3x$ at $x = 2$: $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 2x + 3 = 7$ and $y = 10$. Tangent: $y - 10 = 7(x - 2)$, i.e. $y = 7x - 4$.

A **normal** is perpendicular to the tangent, with gradient $-\dfrac{1}{m}$.

## Stationary points

A **stationary point** has gradient zero: $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 0$.

To find its nature, look at the gradient on either side, or use the second derivative $\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}$:

| $\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}$ | Nature |
|---|---|
| positive | **minimum** |
| negative | **maximum** |

## Worked example

Find and classify the stationary point of $y = x^2 - 6x + 5$.

$$\frac{dy}{dx} = 2x - 6 = 0 \Rightarrow x = 3 \qquad y = 9 - 18 + 5 = -4$$

$\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2} = 2 > 0$, so $(3, -4)$ is a **minimum**.

<Tip>
In optimisation problems (greatest area, smallest cost) write an expression for the quantity, differentiate, set to zero, then check it is a maximum or minimum.
</Tip>
