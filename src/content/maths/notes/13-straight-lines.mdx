# Straight lines: gradient, equation and length

<Key>
A straight line has a constant **gradient** $m$ (steepness) and crosses the $y$-axis at the **intercept** $c$. Its equation is $y = mx + c$.
</Key>

## Gradient

$$m = \frac{\text{change in } y}{\text{change in } x} = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$$

- Positive gradient: rises left to right. Negative: falls. Zero: horizontal. Vertical lines have undefined gradient.
- Through $(1, 2)$ and $(5, 10)$: $m = \dfrac{10 - 2}{5 - 1} = 2$.

## The equation $y = mx + c$

- Read $m$ and $c$ directly: $y = 3x - 4$ has gradient 3 and cuts the $y$-axis at $-4$.
- Rearrange when needed: $2y = 6x + 8$ becomes $y = 3x + 4$.
- To find the equation given a point $(x_1, y_1)$ and the gradient: $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$.

Line through $(2, 5)$ with gradient 3: $y - 5 = 3(x - 2) \Rightarrow y = 3x - 1$.

## Midpoint and length

Between $A(x_1, y_1)$ and $B(x_2, y_2)$:

$$\text{midpoint} = \left(\frac{x_1 + x_2}{2},\ \frac{y_1 + y_2}{2}\right) \qquad AB = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}$$

The length is just **Pythagoras** on the horizontal and vertical differences.

For $A(1, 2)$ and $B(7, 10)$: midpoint $(4, 6)$ and $AB = \sqrt{6^2 + 8^2} = 10$.

## Parallel and perpendicular lines

- **Parallel** lines have the **same gradient**.
- **Perpendicular** lines have gradients that **multiply to $-1$**: $m_1 \times m_2 = -1$.

<Ext>
If one line has gradient $\tfrac23$, a perpendicular line has gradient $-\tfrac32$ (flip it and change the sign).
</Ext>

<Mistake>
Subtracting coordinates in different orders for $x$ and $y$. Use the **same order** (second − first) for both.
</Mistake>

## Worked example

Find the equation of the line through $A(0, 1)$ perpendicular to $y = 2x + 5$.

The perpendicular gradient is $-\tfrac12$. The intercept is 1, so $y = -\tfrac12 x + 1$.
