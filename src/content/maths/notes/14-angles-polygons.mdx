# Angles and polygons

<Key>
Geometry questions need **reasons**. Always write the rule you used, e.g. "angles on a straight line add to $180^\circ$".
</Key>

## Basic angle facts

| Rule | Statement |
|---|---|
| Angles on a straight line | add to $180^\circ$ |
| Angles around a point | add to $360^\circ$ |
| Vertically opposite angles | are equal |
| Angles in a triangle | add to $180^\circ$ |
| Angles in a quadrilateral | add to $360^\circ$ |
| Exterior angle of a triangle | equals the sum of the two opposite interior angles |

## Parallel lines

A line crossing two parallel lines (a **transversal**) creates:

- **Corresponding** angles: equal (F shape).
- **Alternate** angles: equal (Z shape).
- **Co-interior** angles: add to $180^\circ$ (C or U shape).

<Tip>
Name the rule: "corresponding angles", "alternate angles", "co-interior angles". "Z angles" is not accepted as a reason.
</Tip>

## Triangles

- **Equilateral**: 3 equal sides, all angles $60^\circ$.
- **Isosceles**: 2 equal sides, and the **base angles are equal**.
- **Right-angled**: one angle of $90^\circ$.

## Polygons

For a polygon with $n$ sides:

$$\text{sum of interior angles} = (n - 2) \times 180^\circ \qquad \text{sum of exterior angles} = 360^\circ$$

For a **regular** polygon (all sides and angles equal):

$$\text{exterior angle} = \frac{360^\circ}{n} \qquad \text{interior angle} = 180^\circ - \text{exterior angle}$$

| Polygon | $n$ | Interior angle sum | Each angle (regular) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Triangle | 3 | $180^\circ$ | $60^\circ$ |
| Quadrilateral | 4 | $360^\circ$ | $90^\circ$ |
| Pentagon | 5 | $540^\circ$ | $108^\circ$ |
| Hexagon | 6 | $720^\circ$ | $120^\circ$ |
| Octagon | 8 | $1080^\circ$ | $135^\circ$ |

<Mistake>
Interior and exterior angles are on the **same straight line** and add to $180^\circ$. A common error is to treat them as the same.
</Mistake>

## Worked example

Each exterior angle of a regular polygon is $24^\circ$. How many sides?

$$n = \frac{360}{24} = 15$$
