# Circle theorems

<Key>
Every circle theorem comes with a **name**. Spot which theorem applies, then write its name as your reason.
</Key>

## Parts of a circle

**Radius**, **diameter**, **chord** (line joining two points on the circle), **arc**, **sector**, **segment**, **tangent** (touches the circle at one point).

## The theorems

1. **Angle in a semicircle is $90^\circ$.** A triangle drawn on a diameter with its third vertex on the circle has a right angle at that vertex.
2. **Angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference** (when standing on the same arc).
3. **Angles in the same segment are equal.** Angles subtended by the same arc from points on the same side are the same.
4. **Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add to $180^\circ$.** (Quadrilateral with all four vertices on the circle.)
5. **A tangent is perpendicular to the radius** at the point of contact.
6. **Tangents from an external point are equal in length.**
7. **A line from the centre perpendicular to a chord bisects the chord.**
8. **Alternate segment theorem:** the angle between a tangent and a chord equals the angle in the alternate segment.

<Ext>
The alternate segment theorem (theorem 8) is usually needed only in more challenging Extended questions.
</Ext>

## Useful isosceles triangles

Two radii form an **isosceles triangle**, so base angles are equal. Look for triangles with two radii when finding angles.

<Tip>
Mark **radii** and any right angles on your diagram first. They unlock most circle problems.
</Tip>

## Worked example

$O$ is the centre of a circle. Angle $AOB = 100^\circ$ with $A$ and $B$ on the circle, and $C$ is another point on the major arc. Find angle $ACB$.

Angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference:

$$ACB = 100^\circ \div 2 = 50^\circ$$

<Mistake>
Applying "angle at the centre is twice" when the angles stand on **different arcs**. Both angles must stand on the same arc.
</Mistake>
