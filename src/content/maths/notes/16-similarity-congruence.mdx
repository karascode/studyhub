# Congruence and similarity

<Key>
**Congruent** shapes are identical in shape **and** size. **Similar** shapes have the same shape (equal angles, sides in proportion) but possibly different sizes.
</Key>

## Congruent triangles

Two triangles are congruent if any one of these is true:

| Rule | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **SSS** | three sides equal |
| **SAS** | two sides and the **included** angle equal |
| **ASA** (or AAS) | two angles and a corresponding side equal |
| **RHS** | right angle, hypotenuse and one other side equal |

<Mistake>
"AAA" does **not** prove congruence. Triangles with the same angles can be different sizes: they are only similar.
</Mistake>

## Similar shapes

If shape B is an enlargement of shape A, the **scale factor** $k$ is

$$k = \frac{\text{length on B}}{\text{matching length on A}}$$

Matching sides are **in proportion** and matching angles are **equal**.

Triangle sides $3, 4, 5$ and $9, 12, 15$ are similar with $k = 3$.

## Lengths, areas and volumes

If the **length** scale factor is $k$:

| Quantity | Scale factor |
|---|---|
| lengths | $k$ |
| areas | $k^2$ |
| volumes | $k^3$ |

Examples:
- Length scale factor 3 gives area scale factor $9$ and volume scale factor $27$.
- Areas in the ratio $4 : 9$ means lengths in the ratio $2 : 3$ and volumes in the ratio $8 : 27$.

<Tip>
To go from areas or volumes back to lengths, take the **square root** or **cube root** of the scale factor.
</Tip>

## Worked examples

**1.** Two similar cylinders have heights 6 cm and 9 cm. The smaller has surface area $80\ \text{cm}^2$. Find the larger surface area.

$$k = \tfrac{9}{6} = 1.5 \qquad \text{area} = 80 \times 1.5^2 = 180\ \text{cm}^2$$

**2.** A model ship is $\tfrac{1}{50}$ of the real size. Its sail area is $0.4\ \text{m}^2$. The real sail area is

$$0.4 \times 50^2 = 1000\ \text{m}^2$$
