# Perimeter, area and volume

<Key>
**Perimeter** is a length (cm). **Area** is a surface (cm²). **Volume** is space (cm³). Check your units: they should match the type of quantity.
</Key>

## Area of 2D shapes

| Shape | Area |
|---|---|
| Rectangle | $l \times w$ |
| Triangle | $\tfrac12 b h$ |
| Parallelogram | $b \times h$ |
| Trapezium | $\tfrac12 (a + b) h$ |
| Circle | $\pi r^2$ |

The circumference of a circle is $C = 2\pi r = \pi d$.

## Arcs and sectors

For a sector with angle $\theta$ at the centre:

$$\text{arc length} = \frac{\theta}{360} \times 2\pi r \qquad \text{sector area} = \frac{\theta}{360} \times \pi r^2$$

A sector with $r = 6$ and $\theta = 60^\circ$ has arc length $\dfrac{60}{360} \times 12\pi = 2\pi \approx 6.28$ and area $\dfrac{60}{360} \times 36\pi = 6\pi \approx 18.8$.

## 3D shapes

| Solid | Volume | Curved / total surface area |
|---|---|---|
| **Prism** (any uniform cross-section) | area of cross-section × length | add the faces |
| **Cuboid** | $lwh$ | $2(lw + lh + wh)$ |
| **Cylinder** | $\pi r^2 h$ | curved $2\pi r h$; total $2\pi r h + 2\pi r^2$ |
| **Cone** | $\tfrac13 \pi r^2 h$ | curved $\pi r l$ ($l$ = slant height) |
| **Pyramid** | $\tfrac13 \times \text{base area} \times h$ | add the faces |
| **Sphere** | $\tfrac43 \pi r^3$ | $4\pi r^2$ |

The **slant height** of a cone: $l = \sqrt{r^2 + h^2}$.

<Tip>
These formulae are on the formula sheet in many papers, but you must know **how to use** them and choose the right one.
</Tip>

<Mistake>
Using the diameter in place of the radius. Halve the diameter first. Also keep units consistent: convert everything to cm (or m) before calculating.
</Mistake>

## Compound shapes

Split into simple shapes, find each area or volume, then **add** (or **subtract** for a hole).

## Worked examples

**1.** A cylinder has radius 5 cm and height 12 cm.
$$V = \pi \times 5^2 \times 12 = 300\pi \approx 942\ \text{cm}^3$$

**2.** A cone has base radius 6 cm and height 8 cm. Slant height $= \sqrt{36 + 64} = 10$.
$$\text{curved area} = \pi \times 6 \times 10 = 60\pi \approx 188\ \text{cm}^2$$

**3.** A sphere has volume $\tfrac43 \pi r^3 = 288\pi$. Then $r^3 = 216$ and $r = 6$.
