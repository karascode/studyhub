# Right-angled triangle trigonometry

<Key>
Label the sides **from the angle you are using**: the **hypotenuse** (opposite the right angle), the **opposite** (across from the angle) and the **adjacent** (next to the angle).
</Key>

## SOH CAH TOA

$$\sin\theta = \frac{\text{opp}}{\text{hyp}} \qquad \cos\theta = \frac{\text{adj}}{\text{hyp}} \qquad \tan\theta = \frac{\text{opp}}{\text{adj}}$$

## Pythagoras' theorem

$$a^2 + b^2 = c^2$$

where $c$ is the hypotenuse. Use it when you know two sides and want the third, with no angle involved.

## Finding a side

Choose the ratio that links the side you want and the side you know.

Example: angle $35^\circ$, hypotenuse $12\ \text{cm}$, find the side opposite the angle.

$$\sin 35^\circ = \frac{x}{12} \;\Rightarrow\; x = 12 \sin 35^\circ = 6.88\ \text{cm}$$

## Finding an angle

Use the **inverse** function on your calculator.

Example: opposite $= 5$, adjacent $= 8$.

$$\tan\theta = \frac{5}{8} \;\Rightarrow\; \theta = \tan^{-1}(0.625) = 32.0^\circ$$

## Angles of elevation and depression

Measured from the **horizontal**: elevation is *up* from the horizontal, depression is *down* from the horizontal. Draw a sketch and put the right angle in first.

<Tip>
Check your calculator is in **degrees** mode (D or DEG). This is the most common cause of wrong trig answers.
</Tip>

<Mistake>
Rounding too early. Keep full calculator values through the working and round only the final answer to 3 significant figures (angles to 1 decimal place) unless told otherwise.
</Mistake>
