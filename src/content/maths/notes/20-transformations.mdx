# Transformations

<Key>
A transformation moves or changes a shape. The original is the **object** and the result is the **image**. To **describe** a transformation you must state the **type** and give **all** the details it needs.
</Key>

## Describing each transformation

| Transformation | You must give | Shape changes? |
|---|---|---|
| **Reflection** | the **mirror line** (e.g. $x = 2$, $y = x$) | congruent |
| **Rotation** | **angle**, **direction** (clockwise or anticlockwise), **centre** | congruent |
| **Translation** | the **column vector** $\begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix}$ | congruent |
| **Enlargement** | the **scale factor** and the **centre** | similar (size changes) |

Only **reflection** reverses orientation. Rotation, translation and enlargement keep it.

<Mistake>
Describing a transformation with two types at once, such as "rotate and move". Each answer must be **one** transformation with all its details.
</Mistake>

## Reflection

Each point is the same distance from the mirror line as its image, on the opposite side. For the line $y = x$, swap the coordinates: $(2, 5) \to (5, 2)$. For the $x$-axis, change the sign of $y$: $(2, 5) \to (2, -5)$.

## Rotation

- A $180^\circ$ turn needs no direction.
- Rotation about the origin by $90^\circ$ **anticlockwise**: $(x, y) \to (-y, x)$.
- To find the centre, join matching points, draw the **perpendicular bisectors**: they meet at the centre.

## Translation

Every point moves the same amount. $\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix}$ means 3 to the right, 2 down.

## Enlargement

Multiply the distance of each point from the centre by the scale factor $k$.

- $k > 1$: image is bigger. $0 < k < 1$: image is smaller (a **reduction**).
- Lengths multiply by $k$, areas by $k^2$.

<Ext>
A **negative scale factor** puts the image on the **opposite side** of the centre (and inverted). With $k = -2$, the image is twice as large, on the other side.

A **stretch** with scale factor $k$ and an invariant line (e.g. the $y$-axis) changes lengths in **one direction** only.
</Ext>

## Worked example

Triangle $A$ has vertices $(1,1)$, $(3,1)$, $(1,2)$. Enlarge it with scale factor 2, centre the origin.

Multiply each coordinate by 2: $(2,2)$, $(6,2)$, $(2,4)$.

<Tip>
Use tracing paper for rotations. And always check **orientation** and **size** against the original before choosing the transformation.
</Tip>
