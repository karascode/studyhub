# Vectors

<Key>
A **vector** has **size and direction**. A **scalar** has size only. Vectors can be written as column vectors $\begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix}$, or as a letter in bold or with an arrow, $\mathbf{a}$ or $\overrightarrow{AB}$.
</Key>

## Column vectors

$\begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ -3 \end{pmatrix}$ means 4 units right and 3 units down.

If $A = (1, 5)$ and $B = (6, 2)$ then
$$\overrightarrow{AB} = \begin{pmatrix} 6 - 1 \\ 2 - 5 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 5 \\ -3 \end{pmatrix}$$

Going the other way gives the **negative**: $\overrightarrow{BA} = -\overrightarrow{AB}$.

## Adding, subtracting and scalar multiples

Work with each component:

$$\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 6 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix} \qquad 3\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 6 \\ -3 \end{pmatrix}$$

- **Parallel** vectors are scalar multiples of each other.
- Adding vectors tip-to-tail gives the **resultant**.

## Magnitude

The length of $\begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix}$ is found with Pythagoras:

$$\left|\begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix}\right| = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$$

Example: $\left|\begin{pmatrix} 5 \\ 12 \end{pmatrix}\right| = 13$.

## Vector geometry

Express routes between points in terms of known vectors, using $\overrightarrow{AB} = \mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a}$ when $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ are the **position vectors** of $A$ and $B$.

- The **midpoint** $M$ of $AB$: $\overrightarrow{OM} = \tfrac12(\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b})$.
- If $\overrightarrow{AB} = k\,\overrightarrow{CD}$, the lines are **parallel**.
- If $\overrightarrow{AB} = k\,\overrightarrow{AC}$ (sharing a point), then $A$, $B$, $C$ lie on a **straight line**.

<Tip>
For vector geometry problems: find a path from the start to the end using vectors you know, going along the route and writing each step. Replace backwards steps with a minus sign.
</Tip>

<Mistake>
Writing $\overrightarrow{AB} = \mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b}$. It is **end minus start**: $\mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a}$.
</Mistake>

## Worked example

$\overrightarrow{OA} = \mathbf{a}$ and $\overrightarrow{OB} = \mathbf{b}$. $M$ is the midpoint of $AB$. Find $\overrightarrow{OM}$.

$$\overrightarrow{AB} = \mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a} \;\Rightarrow\; \overrightarrow{OM} = \mathbf{a} + \tfrac12(\mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a}) = \tfrac12(\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b})$$
