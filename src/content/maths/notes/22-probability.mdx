# Probability

<Key>
Probability measures how likely an event is, from $0$ (impossible) to $1$ (certain). Write it as a **fraction, decimal or percentage**, never as a ratio or words like "1 in 5".
</Key>

## Basic probability

$$P(\text{event}) = \frac{\text{number of favourable outcomes}}{\text{total number of equally likely outcomes}}$$

- $P(\text{not } A) = 1 - P(A)$.
- All the probabilities in a sample space add to 1.
- **Expected number** $= P \times \text{number of trials}$. If $P(\text{six}) = \tfrac16$, expect 20 sixes in 120 rolls.

## Relative frequency

$$\text{relative frequency} = \frac{\text{number of times it happens}}{\text{total number of trials}}$$

It is an **estimate** of probability from an experiment. More trials give a better estimate.

## Combined events

- **Mutually exclusive** (cannot both happen): $P(A \text{ or } B) = P(A) + P(B)$.
- **Independent** (one does not affect the other): $P(A \text{ and } B) = P(A) \times P(B)$.

"**OR**" means **add**; "**AND**" means **multiply**.

A fair coin and a fair die: $P(\text{head and 6}) = \tfrac12 \times \tfrac16 = \tfrac{1}{12}$.

## Tree diagrams

Draw one set of branches for each event, label each branch with its probability. Multiply **along** the branches, add **between** outcomes. Each pair of branches from a point adds to 1.

**Without replacement** (probabilities change): a bag has 3 red and 2 blue counters. Two are taken without replacing. $P(\text{both red}) = \tfrac35 \times \tfrac24 = \tfrac{6}{20} = \tfrac{3}{10}$.

<Mistake>
Forgetting that without replacement the **denominator drops by 1**, and the number of the colour just taken also drops.
</Mistake>

## Venn diagrams and probability

Fill in the Venn diagram with counts, then use $P(A) = n(A)/n(\xi)$.

<Ext>
**Conditional probability** $P(B \mid A)$ is the probability of $B$ **given that** $A$ has happened. Restrict attention to $A$: $P(B \mid A) = \dfrac{n(A \cap B)}{n(A)}$.

Of 40 students, 25 study French and 10 of those also study Spanish. $P(\text{Spanish} \mid \text{French}) = \tfrac{10}{25} = \tfrac25$.
</Ext>

## Worked example

A bag contains 4 red and 6 green balls. Two are drawn **with replacement**. $P(\text{one of each colour})$:

$$P(RG) + P(GR) = \tfrac{4}{10}\times\tfrac{6}{10} + \tfrac{6}{10}\times\tfrac{4}{10} = 0.24 + 0.24 = 0.48$$
