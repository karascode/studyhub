# Averages, charts and correlation

<Key>
An **average** is a typical value. The **mean**, **median** and **mode** are different averages; the **range** measures how spread out the data are.
</Key>

## The four measures

| Measure | How to find it |
|---|---|
| **Mean** | add all values, divide by how many |
| **Median** | middle value when ordered ($\tfrac{n+1}{2}$th value) |
| **Mode** | most common value |
| **Range** | largest − smallest |

Data: $3, 5, 5, 8, 9, 12$. Mean $= 42/6 = 7$, median $= (5+8)/2 = 6.5$, mode $= 5$, range $= 9$.

- The **mean** uses all the data but is affected by **outliers**.
- The **median** is not affected by extreme values.
- The **mode** is the only average that works for non-numerical data.

## Frequency tables

For values $x$ with frequencies $f$:

$$\text{mean} = \frac{\sum fx}{\sum f}$$

| $x$ | $f$ | $fx$ |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 4 | 4 |
| 2 | 6 | 12 |
| 3 | 10 | 30 |
| Total | 20 | 46 |

Mean $= 46/20 = 2.3$. The median is the $\tfrac{20+1}{2} = 10.5$th value (between the 10th and 11th): both are 3, so the median is 3.

## Grouped data

Use the **midpoint** of each class as $x$. The result is an **estimate** of the mean. The **modal class** is the class with the highest frequency. You can only identify the class containing the median, not its exact value.

<Tip>
In a grouped frequency table with classes like $10 \le x < 20$, the midpoint is $15$. Use $fx$ with the midpoint.
</Tip>

## Charts

- **Pie chart:** angle $= \dfrac{\text{frequency}}{\text{total}} \times 360^\circ$.
- **Bar chart / pictogram / stem-and-leaf:** a stem-and-leaf shows every value and the shape; include a **key**.

<Ext>
**Histogram** with unequal class widths: the **area** of each bar is the frequency. The vertical axis is **frequency density** $= \dfrac{\text{frequency}}{\text{class width}}$.
</Ext>

## Scatter graphs and correlation

A scatter graph shows two sets of data.

- **Positive correlation:** as one increases, the other increases.
- **Negative correlation:** as one increases, the other decreases.
- **No correlation:** no pattern.
- A **line of best fit** passes close to the points with roughly equal numbers above and below. Use it to **estimate** values within the data range.

<Mistake>
Saying correlation proves one thing **causes** the other. Correlation shows a relationship only. Also do not extrapolate far outside the range of the data.
</Mistake>

## Worked example

The mean of 5 numbers is 8. A sixth number is added and the mean becomes 9. Find the sixth number.

Total of first five $= 40$. New total $= 6 \times 9 = 54$. Sixth number $= 54 - 40 = 14$.
