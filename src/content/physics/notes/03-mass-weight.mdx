# Mass and weight

<Key>
**Mass** is the amount of matter in an object. It is measured in **kilograms** and it never changes with location.
**Weight** is the **gravitational force** acting on an object's mass. It is a **force**, measured in **newtons (N)**, and it depends on where you are.
</Key>

## The relationship

$$W = m g$$

- $W$ is weight (N), $m$ is mass (kg), $g$ is the **gravitational field strength** (N/kg).
- On Earth $g \approx 10\ \text{N/kg}$. This is the same quantity as the free-fall acceleration in m/s².
- On the Moon $g \approx 1.6\ \text{N/kg}$, so the same astronaut weighs about one sixth as much but has exactly the same mass.

| | Mass | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | amount of matter | force of gravity on the mass |
| Unit | kg | N |
| Scalar or vector | scalar | vector (points towards the centre of the planet) |
| Changes with place? | no | yes |
| Measured with | balance | newton-meter (spring balance) |

## Gravitational field strength

A **gravitational field** is a region where a mass experiences a force because of another mass. The field strength $g$ is the force on each kilogram:

$$g = \frac{W}{m}$$

## Measuring mass and weight

- A **balance** compares your object's mass with known masses. It gives the same reading anywhere, because both sides are weakened equally.
- A **spring balance / newton-meter** measures weight. It would read less on the Moon.

## Mass and inertia

The greater an object's mass, the harder it is to change its motion. This resistance to a change in motion is called **inertia**. A loaded lorry needs more force than an empty one to get the same acceleration (see forces and $F = ma$).

<Mistake>
"My weight is 60 kg." In everyday life people say this, but in physics **weight is a force in newtons**. A 60 kg person weighs $60 \times 10 = 600\ \text{N}$ on Earth.
</Mistake>

<Tip>
If a question gives mass and asks for weight, multiply by $g$. If it gives weight and asks for mass, divide by $g$. Use $g = 10\ \text{N/kg}$ unless told otherwise.
</Tip>

## Worked examples

**1.** Find the weight of a $4.5\ \text{kg}$ bag on Earth.
$$W = mg = 4.5 \times 10 = 45\ \text{N}$$

**2.** An object weighs $48\ \text{N}$ on the Moon where $g = 1.6\ \text{N/kg}$. Its mass is
$$m = \frac{W}{g} = \frac{48}{1.6} = 30\ \text{kg}$$
