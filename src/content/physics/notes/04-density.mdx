# Density

<Key>
**Density** is the mass of a substance per unit volume. Dense materials pack a lot of mass into a small space.
</Key>

$$\rho = \frac{m}{V} \qquad m = \rho V \qquad V = \frac{m}{\rho}$$

- $\rho$ (rho) is density in $\text{kg/m}^3$ or $\text{g/cm}^3$, $m$ is mass, $V$ is volume.
- $1\ \text{g/cm}^3 = 1000\ \text{kg/m}^3$.
- Water: $1\ \text{g/cm}^3$ ($1000\ \text{kg/m}^3$). Air: about $1.2\ \text{kg/m}^3$. Iron: about $7.9\ \text{g/cm}^3$.
- $1\ \text{ml} = 1\ \text{cm}^3$ and $1\ \text{litre} = 1000\ \text{cm}^3$.

## Floating and sinking

- An object **floats** in a liquid if its density is **less than** the liquid's density.
- It **sinks** if its density is **greater**.
- An object with the same density as the liquid hangs suspended in it.

Ice floats on water because ice ($0.92\ \text{g/cm}^3$) is less dense than water. Oil floats on water for the same reason.

## Practical 1: regular solid

1. Measure the mass $m$ with a balance.
2. Measure length, width and height with a ruler (or the diameter if it is a cylinder) and calculate the volume.
3. Calculate $\rho = m/V$.

## Practical 2: irregular solid

1. Measure the mass with a balance.
2. Part-fill a measuring cylinder with water and record the volume $V_1$.
3. Lower the object in carefully so that it is fully submerged and record $V_2$.
4. Volume of object $= V_2 - V_1$ (the **displacement** method). For a large object use a **displacement (eureka) can** and collect the overflow.
5. Calculate $\rho = m/V$.

## Practical 3: liquid

1. Place an empty measuring cylinder on a balance and set the reading to zero (tare), or record its mass.
2. Pour in liquid and read the volume at the bottom of the meniscus.
3. Read the mass of the liquid from the balance (or subtract the cylinder's mass).
4. Calculate $\rho = m/V$.

<Tip>
Eye level with the meniscus avoids a parallax error. Repeating a measurement and averaging reduces the effect of random errors.
</Tip>

<Mistake>
Mixing units. If volume is in $\text{cm}^3$ and mass in kg, convert first. Stay with g and $\text{cm}^3$, or kg and $\text{m}^3$. Also remember a block's volume must be calculated from all three lengths in the **same** unit.
</Mistake>

## Worked examples

**1.** A metal block has mass $540\ \text{g}$ and volume $200\ \text{cm}^3$.
$$\rho = \frac{540}{200} = 2.7\ \text{g/cm}^3$$

**2.** Find the mass of $0.50\ \text{m}^3$ of concrete of density $2400\ \text{kg/m}^3$.
$$m = \rho V = 2400 \times 0.50 = 1200\ \text{kg}$$

**3.** A stone placed in a measuring cylinder raises the level from $40\ \text{cm}^3$ to $52\ \text{cm}^3$. Its mass is $30\ \text{g}$.
$$V = 12\ \text{cm}^3 \qquad \rho = \frac{30}{12} = 2.5\ \text{g/cm}^3$$
