# Effects of forces

<Key>
A **force** is a push or a pull. It is a **vector** measured in newtons (N). A force can change an object's **speed**, **direction** or **shape**.
</Key>

## Resultant force

The **resultant force** is the single force that has the same effect as all the forces acting on the object.

- Forces in the same direction: **add**.
- Forces in opposite directions: **subtract**.
- **Balanced forces** (resultant zero): the object stays at rest or keeps moving at constant velocity.
- **Unbalanced forces** (non-zero resultant): the object accelerates (changes speed and/or direction).

## Newton's laws (in words you need)

1. **First law.** An object stays at rest or moves at constant velocity unless a resultant force acts on it. (Mass gives it **inertia**.)
2. **Second law.** A resultant force causes acceleration in the direction of the force.

$$F = m a$$

where $F$ is the resultant force (N), $m$ the mass (kg) and $a$ the acceleration (m/s²). One newton gives a 1 kg mass an acceleration of 1 m/s².

3. **Third law.** When object A pushes on object B, B pushes back on A with an **equal and opposite** force. These two forces act on **different** objects.

<Mistake>
Newton's third-law pair never act on the same object, so they never cancel. The "equal and opposite" forces that balance (like weight and the contact force from the ground) are *not* a third-law pair.
</Mistake>

## Friction and drag

**Friction** is a force that opposes motion between surfaces in contact. It acts **opposite** to the direction of motion and transfers kinetic energy to thermal energy (surfaces warm up).

- Reduced by lubricants, smoother surfaces, wheels, air cushions.
- **Air resistance (drag)** is friction on an object moving through a fluid. It increases with speed and with surface area.

## Hooke's law

A spring stretches in proportion to the force applied, up to a limit.

$$F = k x$$

- $F$ is the force (N), $x$ is the **extension** (m) (new length minus original length), $k$ is the **spring constant** (N/m). A stiffer spring has a bigger $k$.
- On a force–extension graph the straight line through the origin shows **direct proportion**. Its gradient is $k$.
- Beyond the **limit of proportionality** the line curves and the spring stretches more for each extra newton. Beyond the **elastic limit** it will not return to its original length.

<Tip>
Extension is the **increase** in length, not the total length. A 12 cm spring stretched to 15 cm has an extension of 3 cm (0.03 m).
</Tip>

## Circular motion

An object moving in a circle at constant speed is **constantly accelerating**, because its **direction** (so its velocity) is always changing. It needs a **resultant force towards the centre** of the circle, called the **centripetal force**.

- Provided by tension in a string, friction (car turning), gravity (planets and satellites), or a contact force.
- The force acts at right angles to the velocity, so it changes direction but **not speed**.
- A bigger mass, higher speed or smaller radius needs a bigger centripetal force.

<Ext>
Cancel the centripetal force (cut the string) and the object moves off in a **straight line along the tangent**, in line with its velocity at that moment. This is Newton's first law.
</Ext>

## Worked examples

**1.** A $1200\ \text{kg}$ car has a driving force of $3000\ \text{N}$ and a total resistance of $1200\ \text{N}$.
$$F = 3000 - 1200 = 1800\ \text{N} \qquad a = \frac{F}{m} = \frac{1800}{1200} = 1.5\ \text{m/s}^2$$

**2.** A spring has $k = 40\ \text{N/m}$. A force of $6\ \text{N}$ gives an extension of
$$x = \frac{F}{k} = \frac{6}{40} = 0.15\ \text{m}$$
