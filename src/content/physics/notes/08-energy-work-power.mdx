# Energy, work and power

<Key>
Energy cannot be created or destroyed. It is only **transferred** from one store to another or **dissipated** (spread out as less useful thermal energy). This is the **principle of conservation of energy**.
</Key>

## Energy stores

| Store | Example |
|---|---|
| **Kinetic** | a moving car |
| **Gravitational potential** | a book on a shelf, water behind a dam |
| **Chemical** | food, fuel, batteries |
| **Elastic (strain)** | a stretched spring or elastic band |
| **Nuclear** | the nucleus of uranium |
| **Thermal (internal)** | a hot drink |
| **Electrostatic** | charged objects |
| **Magnetic** | magnets |

## Energy transfers

Energy moves between stores by **mechanical work**, **electrical work**, **heating** (conduction, convection, radiation) and by **waves** (light and sound).

Examples: a falling ball (gravitational → kinetic), a loudspeaker (electrical → sound), a bulb (electrical → light + thermal), a battery-powered motor (chemical → electrical → kinetic).

## Kinetic and potential energy

$$E_k = \tfrac{1}{2} m v^2 \qquad\qquad E_p = m g h$$

- $E_k$: kinetic energy (J), $m$: mass (kg), $v$: speed (m/s).
- $E_p$: **change in** gravitational potential energy (J), $g = 10\ \text{N/kg}$, $h$: change in height (m).

Doubling the speed **quadruples** the kinetic energy, because of the $v^2$.

<Tip>
A common link: a ball dropped from height $h$ converts $mgh$ into $\tfrac12 mv^2$. If there is no air resistance, $v = \sqrt{2gh}$. The mass cancels, so heavy and light objects reach the ground at the same speed.
</Tip>

## Work done

Work is done when a force moves an object. It is the amount of energy transferred.

$$W = F d$$

where $F$ is the force (N) and $d$ is the distance moved **in the direction of the force** (m). Unit: **joule (J)**. $1\ \text{J} = 1\ \text{N m}$.

Carrying a bag horizontally at constant speed does **no work** on the bag, because the force (upwards) is at right angles to the movement.

## Power

$$P = \frac{W}{t} = \frac{E}{t}$$

Power is the **rate of transferring energy** (or doing work), in **watts (W)**. $1\ \text{W} = 1\ \text{J/s}$.

## Efficiency

$$\text{efficiency} = \frac{\text{useful energy output}}{\text{total energy input}} \times 100\% = \frac{\text{useful power output}}{\text{total power input}} \times 100\%$$

Energy that is not useful is **wasted**, usually as thermal energy in the surroundings. Efficiency can never be over 100%.

Ways to improve efficiency: lubricate moving parts, insulate to reduce heat loss, use LED lamps in place of filament lamps.

<Mistake>
Efficiency above 100%, or a negative energy value. If you get one, you have swapped output and input.
</Mistake>

## Sankey diagrams

A **Sankey diagram** shows energy transfers as arrows whose **width is proportional to the energy**. The wasted energy arrows branch off from the main arrow and the useful output continues.

## Worked examples

**1.** A $2\ \text{kg}$ mass is lifted $3\ \text{m}$ in $4\ \text{s}$.
$$E_p = mgh = 2 \times 10 \times 3 = 60\ \text{J} \qquad P = \frac{60}{4} = 15\ \text{W}$$

**2.** A car of mass $1000\ \text{kg}$ travels at $12\ \text{m/s}$.
$$E_k = \tfrac12 \times 1000 \times 12^2 = 72\,000\ \text{J}$$

**3.** A motor takes in $800\ \text{J}$ and gives $600\ \text{J}$ of useful work.
$$\text{efficiency} = \frac{600}{800} \times 100 = 75\%$$
