# Pressure

<Key>
**Pressure** is the force acting **per unit area**, at right angles to a surface.
</Key>

$$p = \frac{F}{A}$$

- $p$ is pressure in **pascals (Pa)**, $F$ is force (N), $A$ is area ($\text{m}^2$).
- $1\ \text{Pa} = 1\ \text{N/m}^2$. Also $1\ \text{kPa} = 1000\ \text{Pa}$.

## Changing pressure

- **Increase** pressure by increasing the force or **decreasing the area**: knives and nails have thin edges and points.
- **Decrease** pressure by **increasing the area**: tractors have wide tyres, skis spread weight over the snow, snowshoes, a lorry with many wheels.

<Tip>
Use the area in contact with the surface. If an object stands on 4 legs, use the **total** area of all four. Convert $\text{cm}^2$ to $\text{m}^2$ by dividing by $10\,000$.
</Tip>

## Pressure in liquids

A liquid exerts pressure because of the weight of the liquid above. In a liquid:

- pressure **increases with depth**,
- pressure **increases with density** of the liquid,
- at a given depth, pressure acts **equally in all directions**.

That is why a dam is thicker at the bottom, and why submarines are strong, rounded hulls.

<Ext>
The pressure **difference** between two points a vertical distance $h$ apart in a liquid of density $\rho$ is
$$\Delta p = \rho g h$$
For example, $10\ \text{m}$ of water ($\rho = 1000\ \text{kg/m}^3$) gives $\Delta p = 1000 \times 10 \times 10 = 100\,000\ \text{Pa}$, about the same as atmospheric pressure.
</Ext>

## Atmospheric pressure

The weight of air above us produces **atmospheric pressure**, roughly $100\,000\ \text{Pa}$ at sea level. It falls with altitude because there is less air above you. A **barometer** measures it.

## Liquid pressure is transmitted

Liquids are almost incompressible, so a pressure applied to a **confined** liquid is transmitted equally throughout it. This is the principle of **hydraulic** machines (car brakes, jacks).

## Common mistakes

<Mistake>
Confusing force with pressure. A very large force on a large area can give a small pressure; a small force on a tiny point (a pin) can give a huge pressure.
</Mistake>

## Worked examples

**1.** A box weighing $240\ \text{N}$ stands on an area of $0.12\ \text{m}^2$.
$$p = \frac{240}{0.12} = 2000\ \text{Pa}$$

**2.** A girl of mass $50\ \text{kg}$ stands on two shoes, each with area $0.02\ \text{m}^2$.
$$F = mg = 500\ \text{N} \qquad A = 0.04\ \text{m}^2 \qquad p = \frac{500}{0.04} = 12\,500\ \text{Pa}$$

**3.** Rearranging: a pressure of $8000\ \text{Pa}$ over $0.5\ \text{m}^2$ gives a force
$$F = pA = 8000 \times 0.5 = 4000\ \text{N}$$
