# States of matter and the particle model

<Key>
All matter is made of tiny **particles** (atoms, molecules or ions) that are always moving. The **kinetic particle model** explains the properties of solids, liquids and gases by how these particles are arranged, how they move and how strongly they attract each other.
</Key>

## The three states

| | Solid | Liquid | Gas |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Arrangement** | regular pattern, tightly packed | irregular, touching | far apart, random |
| **Movement** | vibrate about fixed positions | move around and slide past each other | move quickly in random directions |
| **Forces between particles** | strong | weaker | almost none |
| **Shape** | fixed | takes shape of container | fills container |
| **Volume** | fixed | fixed | not fixed (fills container) |
| **Compressible?** | no | almost no | yes |
| **Density** | high | high (close to solid) | low |

<Tip>
For "describe" questions give **arrangement**, **motion** and (if asked) **forces**. For "explain" questions link each observed property to the particle behaviour, e.g. "gases can be compressed **because** the particles are far apart with empty space between them".
</Tip>

## Explaining properties

- **Gases fill their container and have low density** because the particles are far apart and move freely.
- **Liquids flow** because particles slide past each other. They are **hard to compress** because the particles are already touching.
- **Solids keep their shape** because particles are held in fixed positions by strong forces, only vibrating.

## Brownian motion

Smoke particles in air, or pollen grains in water, seen under a microscope, **jiggle randomly**. This is **Brownian motion**.

- The visible particles are **hit unevenly** by the much smaller, fast-moving, invisible air or water molecules.
- Each hit is random in direction and size, so the visible particle moves in an irregular path.
- It is **evidence** that fluids are made of moving particles.

<Ext>
Brownian motion also shows that molecules are **very small** (they are not seen) and **move fast and randomly**. A lighter or smaller particle shows more motion; a hotter fluid makes the motion more vigorous.
</Ext>

## Changes of state

| Change | From → to |
|---|---|
| **melting** | solid → liquid |
| **freezing (solidifying)** | liquid → solid |
| **boiling / evaporation** | liquid → gas |
| **condensation** | gas → liquid |
| **sublimation** | solid → gas directly (e.g. dry ice) |

- On **heating**, particles gain **kinetic energy** and vibrate or move more.
- When a solid **melts**, the particles gain enough energy to overcome the forces holding them in position, and they can move around.
- When a liquid **boils**, the particles gain enough energy to break away from each other completely.
- On cooling the reverse happens. Energy is **released** as the substance condenses or freezes.

<Mistake>
Saying "the particles expand when heated". The particles themselves do **not** get bigger. They move **faster** and, for a gas or solid, they move further apart on average, so the **material** expands.
</Mistake>

## Temperature and particle energy

The **average kinetic energy of the particles** determines the temperature of a substance. At a higher temperature the particles move faster on average.

## Worked example

Explain why a gas can be squashed into a smaller volume but a liquid cannot.

- In a gas the particles are far apart with large gaps between them.
- Pushing in the piston reduces the gaps, so the volume decreases.
- In a liquid the particles are already touching, so there is almost no space to reduce.
